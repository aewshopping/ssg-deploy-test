The trawl from last month has resulted in 16 new history books: 16 being a pleasing square number, pleasing because it means the covers stack up nicely for the socials. Even more important than the covers though are the books. In general there is a theme of _culture_ running through them. The three that I will pick out are:

- __Napoleon and his Marshals: Victory, Rivalry, Betrayal__, by _Andrew Roberts_. Never known to write a book under 700 pages long, this latest epic from the pro biographer Roberts may be an interesting new angle on a familiar story. (I started this a couple of days ago so will be writing up the review in a few weeks.)

- __Birth of a Global City: London in a Revolutionary World, 1789-1815__, by _Jerry White_. This combines my home city with my specialist period so I hope to be able to find the time to read this one.

- __Unearthed: Rediscovered poetry from ancient times to the middle ages__, by _Nicholas Bateman_. This looks really interesting, the idea being I think is that all the poems in the book were not found in old books but preserved by chance on fragments of pottery. Hopefully they are good ones.
